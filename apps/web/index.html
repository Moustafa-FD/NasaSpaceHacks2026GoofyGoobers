How we test it (method)
  1. Decode: NASA GIBS tiles are colour-coded. We turn each pixel back into a physical value with GIBS's official colormap. Values are colour-class midpoints of the rendered product (e.g. ±0.3 K for surface temperature), not Level-2 retrievals with quality flags.
  2. Site vs. ring: We average a 3×3-pixel window on the facility and a ring of pixels around it. Subtracting the ring removes regional weather and climate, which leaves the local signal.
  3. Remove seasons: We subtract each calendar month's long-run mean, then average by year.
  4. Trend: Mann-Kendall test (does it go up or down consistently?) and Sen's slope (how fast, per decade).
  5. Before/after: Welch t-test on monthly anomalies, 3 years before the start date vs. after, with sample sizes shrunk for autocorrelation. 95% confidence interval.

p < 0.05 = "significant". Not significant ≠ no effect. Pixels (1 km for heat, 5 km for NO₂) are bigger than most buildings, so small sites get diluted.
